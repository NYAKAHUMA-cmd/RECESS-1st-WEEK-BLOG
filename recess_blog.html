Five Days That Changed How I See Computing

A week-by-week journal of orientation, innovation, GitHub Copilot, and learning to build for real people.

Written by Nyakahuma Cyrus Peter  ·  B34729  ·  Department of Computing, Technology & Engineering  ·  Uganda Christian University

The recess period running from 4th May to 24th July 2026 is not a vacation from academic obligations — it is a structured professional sprint. This blog documents my first five days: the commitments I made, the skills I encountered, and the moments that reminded me why I chose Computer Science.

01
Orientation & The Commitment
Sunday, 4th May 2026

Day one set the tone for everything that followed. We gathered for a full orientation where the structure, expectations, and ground rules of the recess session were laid out clearly. There was no ambiguity about what was expected of us.

The most significant moment of the day was writing our commitment statements — a formal, signed declaration of intent submitted to the Department of Computing, Technology and Engineering. Mine read in part:

I recognise this period as a critical opportunity to advance my technical competencies, not merely a vacation from academic obligations. I will dedicate a minimum of 30 hours per week to deliberate practice. I will build one demonstrable project aligned with IT, version controlled on GitHub with a professional README. My signature below represents a professional contract between myself and the standards of the department.

Writing that statement made the recess feel real. This was not downtime — it was an internship sprint of my own design.

02
Tools, LaTeX & Ground Rules
Monday, 5th May 2026

Day two was about getting operationally ready. We were introduced to the tools we would be using throughout the recess, and a first assignment was issued: write our initial project report using LaTeX — a professional typesetting system that most students hadn't used before. That alone was a signal: we were being trained to work to industry standards.

03
LinkedIn, Innovation & Upshift Ideation
Tuesday, 6th May 2026

Day three was the most packed yet. The theme was Innovation, and multiple facilitators contributed across different sessions.

Mr. Shelby led an eye-opening session on LinkedIn — not just as a platform, but as a professional presence. We explored how to build a compelling profile, what kind of content to post as IT students, and how our online presence is an extension of our portfolio.

Key takeaway: your LinkedIn is not a CV dump — it is a living document of your growth. Post your learnings, your builds, your process. Show people who you are becoming.

The session also touched on exposure — how to market and advertise ourselves as student developers — and professional email communication as the standard channel for all formal correspondence during the recess.

The afternoon shifted to Upshift, a human-centred innovation framework. The day's Upshift context was Ideation. Working in groups, we were tasked with:

Choosing a real problem Linking it to an SDG Writing a problem statement Identifying causes & effects Building a persona Creating diagrams & charts

The energy in the room during group work was remarkable — students debating, sketching on large sheets, arguing about which problem mattered most. It was the first taste of what problem-centred design actually feels like.

04
Presentations, Field Work & Problem Validation
Wednesday, 7th May 2026

Day four was the most physically and mentally demanding day of the week. We began by presenting our group research work from Day 3 — and the facilitators did not hold back. Errors were highlighted and we were sent back to correct them, repeating the cycle until the work met the required standard.

Some of us skipped lunch to complete our corrections. Looking back, that was exactly the right call. The standard mattered more than the comfort.

Strong emphasis was placed on two things in particular: generating a valid problem statement and a clear solution goal. A vague problem produces a vague solution. The facilitators pushed us to be precise.

In the evening, we went out for field work — each group went into the public to validate their identified problem in the real world. The principle was clear: we are not building solutions for ourselves; we are building them for people. Their responses matter.

After returning from the field, every student wrote an individual report based on the public responses gathered, complete with charts and data visualisations. The day ended late, but it ended with something solid in hand.

Upcoming: an AI & GitHub Agentic session announced — attendance compulsory, marks awarded. Venue change from Ankrah hall communicated to all students.

05
GitHub Copilot Dev Days + Cybersecurity Chapter
Friday, 8th May 2026

The final day of the first week was unlike any other — it stepped fully outside the classroom and into the professional tech community. Day five hosted two major events back-to-back.

The morning started with some confusion (a locked lab door, an initial detour to N11A), but I used that time productively — completing my Challenge Statement & Strategic Alignment Report before the session officially opened. A small win.

GitHub Copilot Dev Days — Mukono Edition was presented in partnership with GDG on Campus, with Shakiran Nannyombi (Software Engineer) as lead presenter. The session covered the full evolution and current state of GitHub Copilot.

The "Riding the Waves" framework explained the three generations of AI-assisted development:

2021 – 2023
Wave 1
Code completion. Copilot as a pair programmer.
2024 – Present
Wave 2
Agentic SDLC. Beyond pair programming.
2026 +
Wave 3
Hybrid teams. Humans + AI agents collaborating.

Beyond the evolution story, the session demonstrated Copilot across the full development environment — in VS Code (with Agent Mode, MCP Apps, Subagents, Custom Agents, Claude & Codex integration), in Visual Studio 2026 (with Debugger, Profiler, Azure MCP Server), and on GitHub Mobile for on-the-go collaboration.

GitHub Copilot CLI was also showcased as an AI terminal companion — the ability to write, test, and debug code directly from the terminal, connected to the GitHub MCP Server.

One of the most impressive slides showed the breadth of models now accessible through GitHub Copilot:

The session closed with a Kahoot quiz, awards for top performers, and a sweepstakes for a GitHub Copilot Pro+ one-year subscription for those who completed the survey. I didn't win — but I left knowing things I didn't know in the morning, and that was the real prize.

Evening — Cybersecurity UCU Chapter · First Session

"Guardians of the Byte" — the UCU Cybersecurity Chapter held its inaugural session at the Media Lab, 2:00 PM to 5:00 PM. New vocabulary, new community, new direction. Introduction to Cybersecurity was presented, followed by a Q&A with winners awarded. A world I intend to explore further.

Week One: Complete.

Five days. Two evening sessions. One signed commitment. Countless new concepts. The recess has only just begun, and already the pace feels right — demanding enough to grow, structured enough to stay on track.

The commitment I signed on Day 1 is not a piece of paper in a folder. It is the contract I hold myself to every morning when I open my laptop. 8 is 8. And the work continues.
